FEATURED PROJECT

THE EIGHT-STRING

A MULTI-YEAR BUILD OF A FULLY CUSTOM 8-STRING ELECTRIC GUITAR, FROM A BLANK CAD SKETCH TO A PLAYABLE INSTRUMENT. THEN I BUILT IT ALL AGAIN, THIS TIME WITH EVERYTHING THE FIRST ONE TAUGHT ME.

  • 2 VERSIONS
  • 8 STRINGS
  • FUSION 360 CAD + CAM
  • 3-AXIS CNC

CHAPTER 01 / DESIGN

Start in CAD

Everything began in Fusion 360. I modeled the body thickness, pickup pocket depth, neck pocket tolerances, bridge placement, and the internal cavity routing for the electronics, with the neck and body as separate parts in one assembly so I could check how they fit before cutting any wood.

The goals were simple to state and hard to meet: a design I could actually manufacture on a 3-axis CNC, with room for every piece of hardware, that would still play well.

CHAPTER 02 / MACHINING

From model to wood

I generated toolpaths for both roughing and finishing operations and designed fixtures to hold irregular geometry securely while the machine worked. Grain direction and stress regions drove how I oriented each piece, preserving stiffness and keeping tear-out to a minimum.

The first clamped dry fit was the moment the model became a real object I could pick up.

CHAPTER 03 / VERSION ONE

Strings on, truth revealed

With pickups installed, control circuitry wired, and signal continuity checked, version one was a working instrument. I tested structural stability under full string tension, tuning stability, sustain, and comfort. It held up under load.

It also taught me plenty. Playing it showed me where the ergonomics could be better, where the machining took longer than it should have, and which decisions I would make differently with a second try.

CHAPTER 04 / ITERATION

Back to the model

Version two started as a fresh design, not a patch. Reworking the body shape and the neck and headstock assembly in CAD let me apply what I learned about geometry and stiffness, and simplify the parts so they would be quicker and cleaner to machine.

The model grew to dozens of revisions before I was happy with it.

CHAPTER 05 / MACHINING, AGAIN

Cutting it better

The second time through, the process was smoother. Fixturing was simpler, the toolpaths were more efficient, and the parts came off the machine closer to finished. Bare maple, pickup pockets routed, neck seated: the shape was finally right.

CHAPTER 06 / FINISH

Color, hardware, wiring

A purple stain brought out the grain, frets went in, and then came the hardware and electronics: tuners, bridge, pickups, switch, and controls, all wired up and checked for continuity before the strings went on.
